Blog Post Dashboards: Overview

Share of EU Repository Creation by Country

Recent data shows that while Europe accounts for a substantial share of global developers, its contribution to open-source infrastructure remains fragmented across countries, with activity – as measured by the number of GitHub commits - concentrated to a small number of Member States. Germany alone accounts for roughly one quarter of all active open source projects created in the EU, while Germany and France together represent more than 40% of total European output — reflecting, in part, their larger economic size and industrial base.

Source: Europe’s Digital Future Demands Investment, Not Austerity and Passivity

Share of OS Companies by Country in all EU-27 OS Companies

Analyzing the active companies in the open source sector, the data indicates that France has the largest shares within Europe with 21% of all OS firms being located there, closely followed by Germany with a share of 19%. Spain and the Netherlands follow with a share of 10% each, while and Italy (6%) and Sweden (5%) rank 5th and 6th, respectively. The other EU-27 member states show a rather scattered picture with shares below 5%. From a business perspective, this suggests that open source firms cluster where demand is predictable and sustained, not only where technical talent exists.

Cumulative Open-Source Repositories

The US invests heavily through their domestic BigTech industry further fueling their global platform reach. When ranked among the top ten producing countries, the US consistently stands alone, with a repository base far exceeding that of any other single country.

China has emerged as the strongest individual challenger, reflecting how they treat strategic digital infrastructure as a public-good priority, coupling industrial policy with consistent funding and coordinated state-capital investment, reiterated through their five-year plans. In both cases, continuity of demand, long-term planning, and clear ownership of strategic infrastructure play a central role.

Cumulative Share of Global Open-Source Repositories

Examining development activity provides additional insight and highlights Europe’s underlying strengths. Measured as the total number of commits accumulated across all repositories, the United States accounts for the largest global share (≈32%), followed by the European Union (≈22%), while China represents a smaller, though increasing, share (≈8%).

A similar comparison using contributor data puts EU under a better light. Across all years, the EU accounts for a share of GitHub contributor accounts comparable to that of the United States (≈23%) and substantially higher than that of China (≈12%). That indicates that Europe supplies a large share of the global talent base and does not lack skills or engagement in open-source software.

Professionalization: EU-27 vs United States vs China

The EU-27 is keeping pace and overtaking the US considering organisational embedding intensity, which is defined as the degree to which repositories are concentrated within organisations, rather than dispersed across isolated individual ownership, indicating the professionalisation of the OSS ecosystem, which relates the number of GitHub repositories associated per organisation. This measure thus captures the depth of human capital engagement and distinguishes between ecosystems characterised by many lightly staffed repositories and those with denser collaborative structures embedded in an organisational context.

Compared to China, however, both Europe and the US are clearly lagging behind noting the development until the period 2016-2018. The sudden drop can at least partly be attributed to the rise of Gitee, a very popular DevOps platform launched launched in 2013 by Open Source China (OSChina), a Shenzhen-based open-source software company, which is why the number of repositories as well as the repositories per organisation experienced a decrease when looking at GitHub data only.

Source: The Open Source Community: From Formation to Professionalisation

Professionalization: Repositories per Organization

Increased organisational adoption and professionalisation can be noted in a broader global context. The dashboard points to steady increases in organisational embedding over time, with especially fast acceleration in a subset of emerging ecosystems, especially in India and Brazil where the rise of professionalisation is especially steep. The two outpace both EU-27 and the US, and align more with the levels of China, recognising their drop in the data after 2019.

Maintenance: Commit intensity: The EU-27 in an international comparison

To understand Europe’s standing, it is essential to analyse quantitative metrics such as repository generation, commit frequency, and contributor activity. Data from GitHub and other platforms indicate that while the United States leads in absolute numbers of repositories and contributors, Europe demonstrates a steady growth trajectory.

China has surged in repository creation, especially between 2015 and 2020. Afterwards the activities measurable via GitHub repository formation and commits, however, decreased, which can partly be explained by a shift towards Gitee, a very popular DevOps platform launched launched in 2013 by Open Source China (OSChina).

Source: Productive Yet Pressured: Europe’s Open Source Maintenance Dilemma

Maintenance: Retention rate, EU-27 vs United States vs China

The retention rate captures year-to-year changes in the inflow of contributors entering the ecosystem (Note: The ecosystem is measured based on GitHub data. GitHub is the largest code hosting platform globally with over 100 million developers and hundreds of millions of repositories covered).

It is defined as the ratio of contributors entering in a given year relative to those entering in the previous year, which reflects the capacity of a country’s open-source ecosystem to sustain or expand participation over time.

Maintenance: Retention rate across the EU-27 countries in 2023

Retention rate measures how well a country sustains the inflow of contributors from one year to the next. The top 5 countries in terms of the retention rate are Malta, Denmark, Slovenia, Finland and the Netherlands. Two of these countries, namely Slovenia and Finland score also very high in terms of turnover rates, besides Luxembourg, Bulgaria and Denmark. The graphs points to a fragmented Europe where there is need for harmonised policy and practice for supporting and contributing to the sustainable maintenance of open source technologies.
